Financial Toxicity: The Anatomy of a Medical Shock

An econometric audit of unexpected medical billing variance.

The Billing Black Box

Marcus recovers from a severe diabetic episode, relieved that the physical danger has passed. Then the hospital bill arrives.

Healthcare pricing is notoriously opaque. When we map out standard medical conditions like Diabetes, Asthma, and Heart Disease, the distribution of costs is astonishingly wide. A routine procedure that costs one patient a reasonable deductible can bankrupt another.

Where Do Shocks Happen?

Is this variance just a matter of being uninsured or not? Not entirely.

Looking at the variance matrix, we see that the nature of the admission disrupts standard pricing tiers. When patients enter through Emergency or Trauma pathways, they lose the ability to "shop around." Price transparency vanishes, and the variance in billing across all insurance networks explodes.

The Baseline Cost (2SLS Model)

To separate correlation from causation, we deployed an Instrumental Variable (2SLS) model. The diagnostics are rock-solid (a Wu-Hausman p-value of 0.0000 and a massive F-statistic of 746.54).

The Unexplained Residuals

By calculating the predicted baseline bill and subtracting it from what patients were actually charged, we isolate the Residuals.

The highlighted points on this chart represent the top 1% of statistical anomalies—patients like Marcus. These are "Unexplained Shocks." They cannot be justified by the patient's age, their specific condition, or how many days they stayed in a bed. They represent pure, systemic financial toxicity baked into the pricing architecture itself.

A Note on Data & Governance

The dataset driving this analysis (Nicolybgs/healthcare_data via Hugging Face) is entirely synthetic. Due to HIPAA regulations and strict internal governance, the highly granular patient-level billing and diagnostic data I work with daily cannot be publicly shared.

In the real world, the distribution of costs is influenced by complex, negotiated payer-provider contracts, and the resulting narrative might look different. I built this project using synthetic data specifically to highlight my technical ability to engineer scalable cloud data pipelines, apply rigorous econometric causal inference (2SLS), and translate complex actuarial outputs into human-centric, data narratives.
